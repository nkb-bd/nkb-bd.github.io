# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Three audiences arriving mostly from Google, GitHub, X, and WordPress.org:

- **Founders / hiring managers / CTOs** evaluating whether Lukman can architect
  and lead real product work. They skim for proof and credibility in ~30 seconds.
- **Developers** (WordPress/PHP/Vue) who found a technical article or a plugin,
  and are deciding whether this person is worth following and trusting.
- **Potential consulting / collaboration leads** who need someone who has shipped
  software at scale and can talk about the engineering reasoning, not just features.

The job to be done: quickly understand *who Lukman is professionally, beyond his
current employer*, trust the depth, and take one next step — subscribe, read more,
or make contact.

## Product Purpose

A single, consolidated, long-term professional identity for Lukman Nakib —
independent of any employer. It leverages his work on Fluent Forms and other
WPManageNinja products to build portable reputation (knowledge, architecture
decisions, case studies) that keeps compounding across his career.

It is explicitly a **lead engine**, not a résumé: content that ranks → 30-second
trust → an owned email audience → inbound leads → eventually digital products.
Success = strangers arrive from search, subscribe, and convert to leads/buyers.

The identity hierarchy is deliberate: **Lukman → expertise → products → companies**,
never WPManageNinja → Fluent Forms → Lukman.

## Brand Personality

Expert, plain-spoken, and quietly confident, with a dry wit. The voice of an
engineer who has actually shipped to hundreds of thousands of sites and explains
the *reasoning* — trade-offs, what broke, what he'd do again — instead of hyping.
Three words: **grounded, specific, opinionated.**

Emotional goal: a visitor should think "this is a real person who knows things,"
not "which AI generated this." Authority comes from the work shown (real diagrams,
real code, real numbers), never from decoration.

## Anti-references

- Generic AI-SaaS landing pages: violet/indigo gradients, glassmorphism cards,
  Inter-on-Inter, emoji-bulleted feature grids, fake stat badges, invented
  testimonials, everything centered and symmetrical.
- The **editorial-typographic AI lane**: display serif (often italic) + tiny
  tracked mono/all-caps eyebrows + ruled columns + monochrome restraint. This is
  the second-order reflex — avoid it even though it looks "tasteful."
- The **terminal-developer cliché**: green-on-navy, monospace-everything,
  "Brittany Chiang" template look (this is what the *old* site was).
- Corporate-agency portfolio: stock hero photo + "Elevate your workflow" copy.
- Anything that reads as costume rather than substance.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The site itself is a proof of craft — fast,
   accessible, precise. A WordPress performance/architecture person with a slow,
   sloppy site is a contradiction.
2. **Show the reasoning, not the résumé.** Every case study is problem →
   constraints → architecture → trade-offs → outcome → lessons. Portable knowledge
   beats feature lists.
3. **Real artifacts over decoration.** Diagrams, code, screenshots, and specific
   numbers are the imagery. They're the one thing no generator can fake.
4. **The list is the machine.** Every content page earns the email capture;
   reputation is fuel, the owned audience is the asset.
5. **Independent of the logo.** Foreground Lukman's thinking; companies and
   products are context in his story, not the headline.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast (no light-gray-on-white "elegance"),
large text ≥3:1. Full keyboard navigation, visible focus states, semantic
landmarks. `prefers-reduced-motion` honored on every animation. Light default
theme plus a dark theme toggle. Long-form reading capped at 65–75ch.
